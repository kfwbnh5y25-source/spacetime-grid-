## Abstract

We demonstrate coherent relative-phase gating, threshold control, and nanosecond phase memory between two frequency-detuned parallel-pumping microwave tones in an ensemble of $\sim 12$ nominal $3\;\mu\text{m} \times 50\;\text{nm}$ Bi-substituted yttrium iron garnet (BiYIG) microdisks. When a seed tone at frequency $f_1 \approx 2f_k$ pre-excites a confined spin-wave mode $k$, a four-wave-mixing cross-pump born in $k$ coherently interferes with a second parametric pump at $f_2 \approx 2f_m$ ($\Delta = f_2 - f_1$) driving a target mode $m$ at $f_2/2$. In the sub-threshold scattering regime, the intermodulation idler at $f_1 - f_2/2$ follows the quadratic law $P_{\rm id} \propto a_1^2 P_{f_2/2}$ over more than two decades in seed amplitude $a_1$ (log-log slopes $1.89$–$1.92$), while its coupling constant $C_m$ agrees within $10\text{–}20\%$ with the phase-insensitive quadratic threshold-reduction coefficient $K_{2B}$ ($10\text{–}15\%$ threshold drop at $a_1 = 327\;\text{mV}$). Across the relative carrier phase $\phi$ between the two tones, the $f_2/2$ response exhibits a $2\pi$-periodic modulation $\propto \cos(\phi + \phi_0)$ whose gating depth $1 - P_{\min}/P_{\max}$ reaches $49\text{–}60\%$ ($32\text{–}43\%$ symmetrized contrast) at zero pulse separation (`[PENDING meas3]` precision repeat-averaged depth at fixed operating point). Separating the $300\;\text{ns}$ seed pulse and $400\;\text{ns}$ target pulse by a dark gap $t_{\rm gap} > 0$ reveals that the phase gate survives non-overlapping propagation: the optimal phase $\phi_0$ slews linearly according to $\phi_{\rm eff} = \phi + 2\pi\Delta\, t_{\rm gap}$ (at $70\text{–}83\%$ of $2\pi\Delta$), directly imprinting time-of-flight phase accumulation onto the parametric threshold. Contrary to a single-mode linear memory time $\tau_{\rm mem}^{\rm lin} = (4\pi\Gamma_{k,\rm Hz})^{-1} \approx 49\text{–}53\;\text{ns}$ inferred from the measured Lorentzian half-width $\Gamma_{k,\rm Hz} = 1.50\text{–}1.61\;\text{MHz}$, the measured memory horizon $\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta)$ is strongly detuning-dependent—persisting beyond $7\;\text{ns}$ without decay at $\Delta \le 61\;\text{MHz}$ (`[PENDING meas1]` out to $200\;\text{ns}$ at $\Delta = 50, 58\;\text{MHz}$), collapsing with $\tau_{\rm mem} \approx 2.1\text{–}2.4\;\text{ns}$ near a sharp, cross-dataset second-harmonic $\pi$-jump line at $\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$, and decaying below $1\;\text{ns}$ for $\Delta \ge 80\;\text{MHz}$. Controlled pulse-edge sweeps confirm that gating collapses over a characteristic turn-on ramp scale $\tau_s = 1.7\text{–}1.9\;\text{ns}$ matching both the $\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$ memory time and the single-tone dynamic build-up constant $\tau_b = 2.15 \pm 0.08\;\text{ns}$. We derive a closed-form normal-mode model incorporating the mutual four-magnon nonlinear frequency shift $\tilde\delta_2 = \delta_2 + \Delta^{(4A)}_2$ and phase-sensitive cross-pump $W\cos\Psi$, unifying single-tone Arnold tongues, overlap gating, and time-of-flight phase memory (`[PENDING meas2]` direct threshold-vs-phase fit).

## I. Introduction

Parametric excitation of spin waves by parallel microwave pumping—where an oscillating magnetic field at frequency $f_p \approx 2f_m$ splits a microwave photon into a pair of counter-propagating magnons at $f_m$—is a foundational probe of magnetic relaxation, nonlinear scattering, and magnon condensation [1–3]. In laterally confined geometries such as micron-scale yttrium iron garnet (YIG) and Bi-substituted YIG (BiYIG) disks, dipolar and exchange confinement discretizes the spin-wave spectrum into well-separated standing eigenmodes [4–6]. Recent mode-resolved experiments and micromagnetic normal-mode reductions [5,7–9] have shown that driving two or more confined modes simultaneously with distinct pump frequencies $f_1 \neq f_2$ produces rich nonlinear competition, frequency pulling, self-modulation instabilities, and sequence-dependent (non-commutative) steady states [10–13].

A central prediction of the two-mode normal-modes model (NMM) [10] is that four-magnon interactions between an already-excited seed mode $k$ and a target mode $m$ generate both a phase-insensitive mutual nonlinear frequency shift (NFS) and a phase-sensitive cross-coupling channel. Yet almost all experiments on multi-tone parallel pumping to date—including recent demonstrations of non-commutative steady-state selection in YIG microdisks [11]—rely on overlapping or phase-uncontrolled drives and measure only time- or phase-averaged emitted power. Consequently, two essential questions remain experimentally open: (i) Can the relative microwave phase $\phi$ between two frequency-detuned pumps continuously gate the parametric onset and sub-threshold gain of a target mode $m$, and if so, with what modulation depth? (ii) Does that phase coherence survive a dark propagation interval $t_{\rm gap} > 0$ when the two pump pulses do not overlap in time?

Here we answer both questions in an ensemble of BiYIG microdisks using phase-coherent pulsed two-tone microwave spectroscopy ($64\;\text{GSa/s}$ arbitrary waveform synthesis) coupled with swept spectrum-analyzer (SA) readout of the subharmonic $f/2$ emission. We uncover a coherent **phase-gated parametric memory**:
1. **Quadratic sub-threshold scattering and self-consistent threshold shift:** Below threshold, tone 1 ($f_1 \approx 2f_k$) scatters magnons pumped by tone 2 ($f_2 \approx 2f_m$) into a lower intermodulation sideband at $f_1 - f_2/2$ obeying $P_{\rm id} \propto a_1^2 P_{f_2/2}$ over more than two decades in seed amplitude $a_1$. Adiabatic elimination yields a phase-insensitive threshold reduction $h_{2,\rm th}(h_1) = h_{2,\rm th}(0)(1 - K_{2B}h_1^2)$ whose coupling constant $K_{2B}$ agrees with the scattering coefficient $C_m$ within $10\text{–}20\%$, providing an independent self-consistency test of the four-magnon tensor.
2. **Continuous $2\pi$-periodic phase gating:** Superposed on this phase-insensitive shift, the target emission at $f_2/2$ varies sinusoidally with relative pump phase $\phi$ as $I(\phi) = A_0 + A_1\cos(\phi + \phi_0) + \dots$ with period $2\pi$ (not $\pi$), achieving $49\text{–}60\%$ modulation depth ($1 - P_{\min}/P_{\max}$) at zero pulse gap (`[PENDING meas3]`).
3. **Time-of-flight phase memory and detuning-dependent collapse:** When tone 2 is delayed by $t_{\rm gap} > 0$ after tone 1 turns off, the favorable phase slews linearly as $\phi_{\rm eff} = \phi + 2\pi\Delta\, t_{\rm gap}$ (verified at $70\text{–}83\%$ of the bare $2\pi\Delta$ rate), demonstrating coherent free evolution of the seeded magnon state during the dark interval. Across detuning $\Delta = f_2 - f_1$, the memory lifetime $\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta)$ spans from $>7\;\text{ns}$ at $\Delta \le 61\;\text{MHz}$ (`[PENDING meas1]` out to $200\;\text{ns}$) down to $\tau_{\rm mem} = 2.13 \pm 0.08\;\text{ns}$ on a sharp second-harmonic $\pi$-jump boundary at $\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$ and $<1\;\text{ns}$ for $\Delta \ge 80\;\text{MHz}$.
4. **Turn-on edge-smearing control:** Extending the tone-2 turn-on edge width $t_{\rm edge}$ from $0.5\;\text{ns}$ to $10\;\text{ns}$ extinguishes the phase modulation $A_1/A_0$ from $0.38$ to an $\approx 0.04$–$0.07$ floor on a timescale $\tau_s \approx 1.7\text{–}1.9\;\text{ns}$ while leaving the phase-mean emission $A_0$ unchanged, proving that sharp turn-on relative to seed dynamics is essential to preserve phase selectivity.

Finally, we connect these observations directly to the two-tone NMM equations of d'Aquino *et al.* [10] and differentiate our continuous phase- and gap-resolved memory from the discrete steady-state sequence non-commutativity reported by Soares *et al.* [11]. Throughout this work, following a strict mode-agnostic convention pending full bias-field eigenmode fitting (`[PENDING]` sample characterization), we denote the seed mode excited near $f_1/2$ as $k$ and the target mode excited near $f_2/2$ as $m$, without assigning microscopic mode indices.

## II. Experimental Apparatus and Measurement Protocols

### A. Sample and Microwave Spectroscopy Chain

The device under test consists of an ensemble of $\sim 12$ nominally identical BiYIG microdisks ($3\;\mu\text{m}$ diameter, $50\;\text{nm}$ thickness; saturation magnetization scale $\mu_0 M_s \approx 176\;\text{mT}$ in companion NMM modeling [9,12]) coupled inductively to a microstrip antenna oriented at $30^\circ$ between the RF magnetic field and the static in-plane bias field (`[PENDING]` exact bias-field calibration and broadband FMR eigenmode spectrum for Fig. 1(i)).

Microwave drive waveforms in the $3.76\text{–}4.25\;\text{GHz}$ band are synthesized digitally at $64\;\text{GSa/s}$ ($15.625\;\text{ps}$ sample resolution, 8-bit DAC) using a Keysight M8195A arbitrary waveform generator (AWG) and amplified through a $+35\;\text{dB}$ Mini-Circuits ZHL power amplifier operated strictly in its linear regime (waveform crest capped at $\le 760\;\text{mV}$ DAC amplitude, corresponding to $\le -5\;\text{dBm}$ input ceiling and $<0.1\;\text{dB}$ gain compression; measured third-order intermodulation products $\text{IMD3} \le -31$ to $-41\;\text{dBc}$ on the monitor path). Drive-chain linearity at the excitation port (`full` channel, single-tone calibration) confirms $P_{\rm dr} \propto a^{1.994}$ ($0.43\%$ rms log-residual across $4045\text{–}4085\;\text{MHz}$) and $P_{\rm dr} \propto a^{1.965}$ ($0.64\%$ rms across $3760\text{–}4250\;\text{MHz}$), with no kinks or anomalies across the parametric threshold region.

Subharmonic spin-wave emission near $f/2$ ($1.88\text{–}2.13\;\text{GHz}$) is detected on an Agilent E4440A swept spectrum analyzer (SA) configured in root-mean-square detection and averaging mode (`DET RAV`, `AVER:TYPE RMS`, $1001$ frequency points per trace). For every measurement point, the SA records a baseline trace with the AWG output off and a signal trace with the looped AWG waveform active; all spectral powers are processed in linear power units ($\text{W}$ or $\mu\text{W}/\text{nW}$) after baseline subtraction. Because the M8195A sequences memory in multiples of $1280\;\text{samples}$ ($20\;\text{ns}$), total loop durations are aligned to $20\;\text{ns}$ quanta (typically $1\text{–}2\;\mu\text{s}$), whereas all interior pulse start times, widths, and inter-pulse gaps retain full $1\;\text{sample}$ ($15.625\;\text{ps}$) precision.

### B. Pulse Geometries, Phase Reference, and Data Hygiene

Three complementary waveform geometries are employed:

1. **Single-tone reference sweeps (Fig. 1):** Pulsed $1\;\mu\text{s}$ segments of $a\sin(2\pi f t)$ in a $50\;\mu\text{s}$-on / $3\;\mu\text{s}$-off burst train ($94\%$ duty, `halfaverage` channel) mapped across $f = 3760\text{–}4250\;\text{MHz}$, $a = 200\text{–}500\;\text{mV}$ ($\text{RBW} = 20\;\text{kHz}$, $6630$ points, $67{,}245$ repeats) and zoomed over $f = 4045\text{–}4085\;\text{MHz}$, $a = 250\text{–}350\;\text{mV}$ ($\text{RBW} = 510\;\text{kHz}$, $4188$ points, $76{,}291$ repeats), plus a continuous $50\%$-duty pulse-train length sweep (`3/3.h5`, $f = 4072\;\text{MHz}$, $\tau_p = 0.3125\text{–}9.22\;\text{ns}$, $a = 200\text{–}1000\;\text{mV}$, $\text{RBW} = 300\;\text{kHz}$).
2. **Simultaneous / overlapping two-tone sweeps (Figs. 2, 3, and S1):**
   - *Sub-threshold scattering & threshold reduction (`threshold_reduction/data.h5`, $72{,}920$ groups):* $f_1 \in \{4071, 4081\}\;\text{MHz}$, $f_2 \in \{4125, 4135\}\;\text{MHz}$, comparing delayed timing (`D`, $1000\;\text{ns}$ tone 1 leading $500\;\text{ns}$ tone 2 with $500\;\text{ns}$ overlap) against simultaneous timing (`ND`), read out across five SA channels (`EX`, `f1`, `f2`, lower idler `m`, upper sideband `p`).
   - *Overlap two-phase map (`f1_4069_5_a1_a2_f2_sweep.h5`):* Fixed $f_1 = 4069.5\;\text{MHz}$ across $a_1 \in \{200\text{–}324\}\;\text{mV}$, $a_2 = 200\text{–}275\;\text{mV}$, $f_2 = 4075\text{–}4150\;\text{MHz}$ at two relative phases $\phi \in \{1.31, 4.55\}\;\text{rad}$ ($\Delta\phi = 185.7^\circ$) plus an $a_1=0$ reference. `[PENDING meas3]` replaces this two-phase slice with a 60-phase, repeat-averaged depth scan with fresh per-trial baselines.
3. **Sequential non-overlapping phase-gating and delay memory sweeps (Figs. 4–6):**
   Tone 1 ($a_1 = 327\;\text{mV}$, duration $300\;\text{ns}$) ends at $t = 0$, followed by a dark gap $t_{\rm gap}$ before tone 2 ($a_2 = 588\;\text{mV}$, duration $400\;\text{ns}$) turns on over $[t_{\rm gap}, t_{\rm gap}+400\;\text{ns}]$ inside a $2\;\mu\text{s}$ loop ($1\;\text{ns}$ Tukey edges, corresponding to $64\;\text{samples}$ at $64\;\text{GSa/s}$). Waveforms are synthesized with a common time origin $t_0$:
   $$s_i(t) = \frac{a_i}{1000}\,w_i(t)\sin\!\big[2\pi f_i (t - t_0) + \phi_i\big],$$
   so that the programmed relative phase $\phi = \phi_2 - \phi_1$ is referenced cleanly to $t_0$ and accumulates $2\pi\Delta\,t_{\rm gap}$ across the dark interval independently of segment boundaries. Data span uniform 16-phase grids (`detune_fine.h5`, $4800$ keys; `delay_f1.h5`, $f_2=4142\;\text{MHz}$; `delay_delta.h5`, $\Sigma=(f_1+f_2)/2 = 4106\;\text{MHz}$, $\Delta = 50\text{–}88\;\text{MHz}$), 25-phase cross-checks (`detune copy 2.h5`, $60{,}360$ keys), and high-resolution 60-phase delay scans (`delay.h5` at $\Delta = 61\;\text{MHz}$, `delay2.h5` at $\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$). `[PENDING meas1]` extends $t_{\rm gap}$ to $200\;\text{ns}$ at $\Delta = 50, 58, 64, 70\;\text{MHz}$ using the verified $300/400\;\text{ns}$, $1\;\text{ns}$-edge geometry.

Four strict data-hygiene safeguards are enforced across all analyses: (i) in delay files containing both `1` (sample path) and `1EX` (excitation monitor) readout keys, only `ro = "1"` traces are retained; (ii) in June/July single-sweep files where the SA baseline cache refreshes every $\sim 90\;\text{s}$ against $18\text{–}140\;\text{s}$ shuffled phase blocks, change-point ($|z| > 5$) and soft-$L_1$ outlier filters exclude $3.1\text{–}8.0\%$ step-corrupted blocks (leaving clean-only fits altered by $<1\%$); (iii) truncated legacy HDF5 files (`delay_fine.h5`, $3.1/171\;\text{MB}$; `detune_fine031126.h5`, pass 1 only) are logged and cross-checked against complete datasets (`delay_f1.h5`, `detune_fine.h5`, `delay.h5`/`delay2.h5`); and (iv) all integrated spectral powers in the June/November phase-gating runs are reported in true band-integrated $\mu\text{W}$ (after correcting a legacy $\mu\text{W}$/$\text{nW}$ label mismatch in earlier analysis logs).

## III. Theoretical Model of Two-Tone Phase Gating and Memory

We derive the closed-form phase-gating, threshold-shift, and memory equations directly from the two-mode Normal Modes Model (NMM) of d'Aquino *et al.* [10] [Eqs. (42)–(43), p. 9; derived via the projection formalism of Perna *et al.*, Ref. 9, Eqs. (2)–(5), (40)–(46)], using a unified rate convention throughout.

### A. Single-Mode Threshold and Rate Conventions

In the lab frame, the free-decay equation for complex modal amplitude $b_m(t)$ is $\dot b_m = -(\Gamma_m + i\,2\pi f_m)b_m$. Passing to the rotating frame $b_m \to b\,e^{-i\pi f_p t}$ under a parallel pump $h_p\sin(2\pi f_p t + \phi)$ near $2f_m$ yields
$$\dot b = -(\Gamma_m + i\delta_m)\,b + i V_m h_p\, e^{-i\phi}\, b^*, \qquad \delta_m = 2\pi\!\left(f_m - \frac{f_p}{2}\right), \tag{1}$$
where $\Gamma_m$ is the exponential amplitude relaxation rate (equivalent to $\lambda_h = \alpha\omega_h^2\|\boldsymbol\varphi_h\|^2$ in Ref. [10], Eq. (14)) and $V_m$ is the parametric coupling coefficient defined such that the off-diagonal pump matrix element is $i V_m h_p$ (matching $\omega_h|(\boldsymbol\varphi_h,\boldsymbol\varphi_h^*)|/2$ in Ref. [10], Eq. (24); texts defining a $2\Gamma/V$ threshold absorb a factor of $2$ into $V$). Note the $2\pi$ relation between rate and spectroscopy: a free decay $b \propto e^{-\Gamma_m t}$ produces a Lorentzian power spectrum with half-width at half-maximum (HWHM) in cycles/s given by $\Gamma_{m,\rm Hz} = \Gamma_m/(2\pi)$, so the amplitude lifetime is $\tau_{\rm amp} = (2\pi\Gamma_{m,\rm Hz})^{-1}$.

Diagonalizing the $2\times 2$ linearized system gives eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm = -\Gamma_m \pm \sqrt{(V_m h_p)^2 - \delta_m^2}$, yielding the single-tone parametric threshold (Arnold tongue):
$$h_{\rm th}^{(0)}(\delta_m) = \frac{\sqrt{\Gamma_m^2 + \delta_m^2}}{V_m}, \tag{2}$$
which equals $\Gamma_m/V_m$ at resonance ($\delta_m = 0$) and matches Ref. [10], Eqs. (20) and (24), identically (verified to $3.5\times 10^{-14}$ relative deviation against the full 29-mode NMM solver).

### B. Internal Four-Magnon Cross-Pump and Phase-Gated Threshold

When tone 1 excites seed mode $k$ to complex amplitude $b_k$, four-magnon interactions between $k$ and $m$—governed by the quartic tensor $d_{hijk}$ of Ref. [10], Eqs. (9)–(10) and mutual coupling $\tilde N_{hn}$ in Eq. (40)—introduce two distinct contributions to the linearized equation for target mode $m$:
1. **Mutual 4A nonlinear frequency shift (NFS):** $\Delta^{(4A)}_2 = n_{21}|b_k|^2$ (corresponding to $2\omega_m N_{mk}|a_k|^2$ in Ref. [10], Eq. (50)), shifting the effective target detuning to $\tilde\delta_2 = \delta_2 + \Delta^{(4A)}_2$. As verified against the numerical NMM solver, omitting $\Delta^{(4A)}_2$ when the seed carries power shifts the predicted $16$-phase threshold curve by up to $\sim 13\%$ of $h_{\rm th}^{(0)}$, exceeding the coherent phase modulation itself.
2. **Coherent 4B cross-pump:** A parametric forcing term $\mathcal{F}_m^{(4B)} = P_{mk} b_k^2 b_m^*$, which acts as an internal pump at frequency $2f_k \approx f_1$ with effective rate amplitude $W = |P_{mk}|\,|b_k|^2$ and phase $\phi_W = 2\theta_k + \arg P_{mk}$.

In the rotating frame of tone 2 ($f_2 = f_1 + \Delta$), the total parametric drive on mode $m$ is therefore
$$A(t) = V_m h_2\, e^{-i\phi} + W\, e^{-i(2\pi\Delta\, t + \psi_0)}. \tag{3}$$
For stationary continuous-wave (CW) locking, the beat term requires $|\Delta| \lesssim \Gamma_m$; in pulsed sequences, as discussed below, the instantaneous value of $A$ at tone-2 turn-on $t_{\rm on}$ selects the threshold and initial amplification quadrature. Setting $|A| = \sqrt{\Gamma_m^2 + \tilde\delta_2^2}$ with relative angle $\Psi = \phi - \psi_0$ at turn-on yields a quadratic equation for $V_m h_2$ whose exact positive root is
$$h_{2,\rm th}(\Psi) = \frac{\sqrt{\Gamma_m^2 + \tilde\delta_2^2 - W^2\sin^2\Psi} - W\cos\Psi}{V_m}. \tag{4}$$
In the weak-coupling regime $W \ll \Gamma_m$ relevant to our experiments ($W/\Gamma_m \lesssim 0.04$ in NMM estimates), Eq. (4) linearizes to
$$h_{2,\rm th}(\Psi) \simeq \frac{\sqrt{\Gamma_m^2 + \tilde\delta_2^2}}{V_m} - \frac{W}{V_m}\cos\Psi, \tag{5}$$
making four falsifiable predictions: (i) threshold modulation is sinusoidal with **period $2\pi$** (not $\pi$); (ii) the peak-to-valley threshold swing $2W/V_m \equiv 2h_W$ directly measures $W$ in magnetic-field units without requiring microscopic tensor elements; (iii) the phase-averaged threshold is unchanged to linear order in $W$; and (iv) Eq. (4) matches the full NMM solver's instantaneous threshold to machine precision ($2.1\times 10^{-11}\;\text{mT}$) once $\tilde\delta_2$ is included.

### C. Sub-Threshold Gating Depth vs. Phase-Insensitive Self-Energy

Below threshold ($|A(\Psi)| < \Gamma_m$ at $\tilde\delta_2 = 0$), the steady-state power of the amplified quadrature scales as $P_m(\Psi) \propto P_{\rm seed}\big(1 - |A(\Psi)|/\Gamma_m\big)^{-2}$, yielding a phase-gating ratio
$$\frac{P_{\max}}{P_{\min}} = \left(\frac{1 - x + w}{1 - x - w}\right)^2, \qquad x = \frac{V_m h_2}{\Gamma_m}, \quad w = \frac{W}{\Gamma_m} \quad (x + w < 1). \tag{6}$$
*(We note that projection onto the orthogonal, de-amplified quadrature $(1 + |A|/\Gamma_m)^{-2}$ softens the $\Psi$ contrast to a $\cos^2\Psi$ projection.)* Defining the **phase-gating depth** as $D = 1 - P_{\min}/P_{\max}$, achieving $D \ge 50\%$ requires $P_{\max}/P_{\min} \ge 2$, which is satisfied identically when
$$\frac{w}{1 - x} \ge \frac{\sqrt{2}-1}{\sqrt{2}+1} = 3 - 2\sqrt{2} \approx 0.172 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{4w}{1 - x} \ge 0.686. \tag{7}$$
Thus, even a modest cross-pump $w \approx 0.005\text{–}0.04$ produces $\ge 50\%$ modulation depth as tone 2 approaches threshold ($1 - x \approx 0.02\text{–}0.24$), unifying the threshold and gain pictures under a single control knob.

In parallel, three-wave or detuned four-wave scattering between tone 1 and an off-resonant intermediate idler channel $s_-$ at $f_1 - f_2/2$ produces a **phase-insensitive** self-energy upon adiabatic elimination of the idler:
$$\Sigma_k = \frac{\kappa_{km}\kappa_{mk}^* h_1^2}{\Gamma_{\rm id} + i\delta_{\rm id}}, \qquad h_{2,\rm th}(h_1) = h_{2,\rm th}(0)\left(1 - K_{2B} h_1^2\right), \tag{8}$$
accompanied by sub-threshold idler emission $P_{\rm id} = C_m a_1^2 P_{f_2/2}$ (see Supplemental Material SM-S1). Combining both mechanisms gives an effective damping representation at resonance:
$$\Gamma_{\rm eff}(h_1, \Psi) = \Gamma_m - C(h_1,\delta) - W(h_1)\cos\Psi, \tag{9}$$
cleanly separating the phase-insensitive self-energy shift $C h_1^2 \leftrightarrow K_{2B} h_1^2$ (which survives at any detuning and cannot gate phase) from the coherent cross-pump $W\cos\Psi$ responsible for phase gating.

### D. Propagation Gap: Time-of-Flight Phase Imprint and Memory Law

When tone 1 terminates at $t = 0$ and tone 2 turns on after a dark gap $t_{\rm on} = t_{\rm gap} > 0$, the free-running seed mode $k$ continues to precess at $f_k = f_1/2$. Evaluated at $t_{\rm on}$ in the laboratory frame, the relative angle between the external tone-2 pump ($2\pi f_2 t_{\rm on} + \phi$) and the internal cross-pump ($2\pi(2f_k)t_{\rm on} + 2\theta_0$) accumulates linearly with gap duration:
$$\Psi(t_{\rm gap}) = \phi + 2\pi\Delta\, t_{\rm gap} + \text{const}, \qquad \Delta = f_2 - f_1. \tag{10}$$
Meanwhile, under linear relaxation $b_k(t) = b_k(0)e^{-\Gamma_k t}$, the quadratic cross-pump decays at twice the amplitude rate:
$$W(t_{\rm gap}) = W(0)\, e^{-2\Gamma_k t_{\rm gap}}, \qquad \tau_{\rm mem}^{\rm lin} = \frac{1}{2\Gamma_k} = \frac{1}{4\pi\Gamma_{k,\rm Hz}}. \tag{11}$$
Equation (10) establishes an exact equivalence between programmed carrier phase $\phi$ and physical propagation delay $t_{\rm gap}$—a result we confirm across every dataset—whereas testing the linear decay law (11) reveals striking nonlinear and detuning-dependent departures near mode-pair boundaries, as detailed below.

## IV. Experimental Results

### A. Single-Tone Parametric Landscape and Dynamic Build-Up (Fig. 1)

Figure 1 summarizes the single-tone subharmonic ($f/2$) excitation landscape of the BiYIG microdisk ensemble. Across $f = 3760\text{–}4250\;\text{MHz}$ and calibrated drive power $P_{\rm dr} = -26.0$ to $-16.8\;\text{dBm}$ (Fig. 1(a)), the $3\sigma$ onset boundary ($0.98\;\text{nW}$ threshold in the $\text{RBW}=20\;\text{kHz}$ overview) traces a multi-lobed Arnold-tongue profile with local threshold minima at $3812, 3912, 3934, 4003, 4102, 4151$, and $4218\;\text{MHz}$ ($-23.2$ to $-20.7\;\text{dBm}$), reflecting discrete confined eigenmodes of the microdisks (`[PENDING]` Fig. 1(i) FMR/eigenmode spectrum). At a fixed drive amplitude of $335\;\text{mV}$ ($P_{\rm dr} \approx -20.8\;\text{dBm}$, Fig. 1(c)), the $f/2$ response rises in discrete spectral steps around $4080\text{–}4097\;\text{MHz}$ toward a peak near $4117\;\text{MHz}$ ($\approx 231\;\text{nW}$).

In the high-resolution zoom around the seed operating frequency $f_{\rm exc} = 4065\;\text{MHz}$ ($250\text{–}350\;\text{mV}$, $\text{RBW}=510\;\text{kHz}$, inset of Fig. 1(a) and Figs. 1(d)–(f)), the $f/2$ signal crosses $3\sigma$ ($1.5\;\text{nW}$) at $a_{\rm th} = 260.8\;\text{mV}$ ($-23.11\;\text{dBm}$). While linear-power cuts show a convex slope increase around $a_{k1} = 317.5\;\text{mV}$ (shifting across $4059\text{–}4070\;\text{MHz}$), variance-stabilized logarithmic analysis ($p \approx 0.26\text{–}0.35$) and narrow-RBW spectra (single $\approx 37\;\text{kHz}$ line, Fig. 1(f)) show smooth convex growth without a new resolved spectral branch, while drive-chain calibration (Fig. 1(g)) rules out RF electronics artifacts. Thus, the single-tone sweeps establish a clean, well-characterized operating band ($4065\text{–}4150\;\text{MHz}$) for the two-tone pulse protocols illustrated in Fig. 1(h).

To probe the intrinsic turn-on dynamics independently of two-tone interference, we measure the single-tone threshold $a_{\rm th}(\tau_p)$ at $f = 4072\;\text{MHz}$ under a $50\%$-duty square pulse train of variable pulse width $\tau_p = 0.3125\text{–}9.22\;\text{ns}$ (`3/3.h5`; Fig. 6(f)). The threshold drops from $590\;\text{mV}$ at $\tau_p = 0.31\;\text{ns}$ to a saturated plateau $a_\infty = 320\;\text{mV}$ for $\tau_p \gtrsim 8\;\text{ns}$. Fitting $a_{\rm th}(\tau_p) = a_\infty / \sqrt{1 - e^{-\tau_p/\tau_b}}$ yields a build-up time constant $\tau_b = 2.15 \pm 0.08\;\text{ns}$ (rms $28\;\text{mV}$ over $57$ points), matching onset-restricted ring-up fits ($\tau_r = 2.1\text{–}2.7\;\text{ns}$ at $a = 420\text{–}430\;\text{mV}$). Converting $\tau_b$ into an effective dynamic damping rate gives $(2\tau_b)^{-1} = 232.6\;\text{Mrad/s}$—equivalent to a HWHM of $\Gamma_{\rm dyn}^{\rm [Hz]} = (4\pi\tau_b)^{-1} = 37.0\;\text{MHz}$—nearly $24\times$ faster than the small-signal HWHM ($1.50\text{–}1.61\;\text{MHz}$), demonstrating that driven nonlinear dynamics operate on a few-nanosecond timescale.

### B. Sub-Threshold Idler Scaling and Self-Consistent Threshold Reduction (Figs. 2 and S1)

Turning to two-tone excitation (`threshold_reduction/data.h5`, $72{,}920$ groups), Fig. 2 establishes the microscopic link between four-wave scattering and threshold reduction. Below tone 1's threshold ($a_1 < a_{1,\rm th}$), tone 1 scatters magnons pumped by tone 2 ($f_2/2$) into the lower intermodulation idler at $f_{\rm id,-} = f_1 - f_2/2$ ($2003.5\text{–}2018.5\;\text{MHz}$). Across more than two decades in seed amplitude ($a_1 \ge 25\;\text{mV}$, $a_2 \ge 350\;\text{mV}$), the normalized idler power $P_m / P_{f_2/2}$ follows a clean power law in $a_1$ (Fig. 2(a)) with fitted log-log slopes of $1.888 \pm 0.014$ at $f_2 = 4125\;\text{MHz}$ ($N=1101$) and $1.918 \pm 0.017$ at $f_2 = 4135\;\text{MHz}$ ($N=1085$), confirming the quadratic prediction $P_m = C_m a_1^2 P_{f_2/2}$ (slopes $1.83\text{–}1.94$ across $f_1 \in \{4071, 4081\}\;\text{MHz}$). Across the full $(a_1, a_2)$ plane (Figs. 2(b)–(g)), the measured maps agree quantitatively with $C_m a_1^2 P_{f_2/2}$ (median $\log_{10}$ residual $0.030\text{–}0.032$, rms $0.073\text{–}0.079$). Meanwhile, the mirror upper sideband at $f_{\rm id,+} = f_2 - f_1/2$ obeys $P_p \propto a_2^2 P_{f_1/2}$ above $a_{1,\rm th}$ ($t = +5.9$ to $+11.0$), appearing $\sim 50\times$ weaker simply because $P_{f_1/2} \ll P_{f_2/2}$ at the operating point (SM-S1).

Crucially, independent fits of the sub-threshold scattering coefficient $C_m$ and the phase-insensitive threshold-reduction coefficient $K_{2B}$ (Eq. (8)) agree within $10\text{–}20\%$ across all four $(f_1, f_2)$ pairs (Table I, SM-S1): $C_m = (0.85\text{–}1.50)\times 10^{-6}\;\text{mV}^{-2}$ versus $K_{2B} = (0.94\text{–}1.36)\times 10^{-6}\;\text{mV}^{-2}$ ($R^2 = 0.92\text{–}0.98$), and both scale upward by $+29\text{–}44\%$ when $f_1$ shifts from $4071$ to $4081\;\text{MHz}$. At the working seed amplitude $a_1 = 327\;\text{mV}$, this corresponds to an absolute phase-insensitive threshold reduction of $K_{2B}a_1^2 \approx 10\text{–}15\%$.

In contrast, in the two-phase overlap sweep (`f1_4069_5_a1_a2_f2_sweep.h5`, $f_1 = 4069.5\;\text{MHz}$, $a_1 = 324\;\text{mV}$, Fig. S1), where only two nearly antipodal phases $\phi = 1.31$ and $4.55\;\text{rad}$ were recorded, the $10\;\text{dB}$-gain threshold crossing shifts by $1.33\;\text{mV}$ ($0.53\%$) at $f_2 = 4111\;\text{MHz}$ and at most $5.40\;\text{mV}$ ($2.0\%$) at $f_2 = 4105\;\text{MHz}$ (correcting an earlier script unit error that treated $\text{mV}$ shifts as $\text{dB}$). This small two-point threshold differential confirms that two-point phase sampling at arbitrary $\phi$ provides only a lower bound on the full $2\pi$ modulation.

### C. Coherent $2\pi$-Periodic Phase Gating and Gating Depth (Fig. 3 & Delay $t_{\rm gap}=0$)

To isolate the coherent phase-sensitive gate $W\cos\Psi$, Fig. 3 analyzes the overlapping CW-pulse sweep at $f_1 = 4069.5\;\text{MHz}$, $a_1 = 324\;\text{mV}$ across $(a_2, f_2)$ for $\phi = 1.31\;\text{rad}$ (Fig. 3(a)) and $\phi = 4.55\;\text{rad}$ (Fig. 3(b)). Subtracting the two states yields coherent phase contrast across the resonance band reaching $+4.08\;\text{dB}$ at $(a_2=272\;\text{mV}, f_2=4107\;\text{MHz})$ and $-3.30\;\text{dB}$ at $(235\;\text{mV}, 4117\;\text{MHz})$ (Fig. 3(c)), with the target $f_2/2$ line in Fig. 3(d) suppressed or enhanced well above the $f_2$-only baseline (up to $20.5\;\text{dB}$ total-band / $26.5\;\text{dB}$ peak-metric gain over single-tone drive at $f_2 = 4111\;\text{MHz}$).

Because $\Delta\phi = 185.7^\circ$ is nearly antipodal and sits away from the cosine extrema ($\phi_0 \approx -155^\circ$ in detuned sweeps), a pooled two-phase cosine fit across $1336$ strong cells in Fig. 3(e) recovers only a weak lower-bound amplitude $g = A_1/A_0 = 0.011$ ($|h_{4B}/h_2| \approx 0.005$, $\hat\phi_0 = 77.9^\circ$ with $68\%$ CI $[13^\circ, 160^\circ]$), leaving the full smooth-cosine sufficiency unresolved in that dataset. Resolving the full $2\pi$ phase waveform at $t_{\rm gap} = 0$ on 16-phase sweeps (`delay_delta.h5`, $\Sigma = 4106\;\text{MHz}$, $\Delta = 50\text{–}70\;\text{MHz}$) directly yields a phase-gating depth
$$1 - \frac{P_{\min}}{P_{\max}} = 49\text{–}60\% \qquad \left(\text{symmetrized } \frac{P_{\max}-P_{\min}}{P_{\max}+P_{\min}} = 32\text{–}43\%\right),$$
confirming $\ge 50\%$ peak-to-valley modulation of the idler emission by relative phase alone (`[PENDING meas3]` 60-phase repeat-averaged verification at the fixed operating point, and `[PENDING meas2]` direct $h_{2,\rm th}(\Psi)$ fit to Eq. (4)). Notably, clean $\cos\phi$ modulation is observed in overlap even at $\Delta \approx 58\text{–}64\;\text{MHz} \gg \Gamma_m$, a regime where steady-state CW averaging would wash out the beat term in Eq. (3)—pointing to burst-wise phase re-selection at each $2\;\mu\text{s}$ pulse onset (Sec. V).

### D. Non-Overlapping Phase Memory, Slew Law, and Detuning Dependence (Fig. 4)

Figure 4 demonstrates that phase gating persists when tone 1 ($300\;\text{ns}$) and tone 2 ($400\;\text{ns}$) are completely separated in time ($t_{\rm gap} > 0$). Across the non-overlapping detuning map (`detune_fine031126.h5`, Figs. 4(a)–(c)), the fundamental $2\pi$-periodic harmonic $A_1$ peaks in a hot band around $f_1 \approx 4070\;\text{MHz}$, $f_2 \approx 4135\;\text{MHz}$ ($\Delta = 65\;\text{MHz}$, $2.73\;\text{dB}$ contrast over the off-band median), with high sinusoidal fidelity (median $R^2 = 0.969$) and a stable optimal phase $\phi_0 \approx -155^\circ$ ($10\text{–}90\%$ spread $3.3^\circ$ in the hot band). Simultaneously, the second-harmonic ratio $A_2/A_1$ drops from $0.324$ off-band to $0.129$ inside the hot-band core.

Scanning $t_{\rm gap}$ from negative overlap ($-17.6\;\text{ns}$) to positive non-overlapping gaps ($+13.3\;\text{ns}$) at $f_2 = 4142\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay_f1.h5`, Fig. 4(d)) reveals two central signatures:
1. **Coherent time-of-flight phase accumulation:** For $t_{\rm gap} \ge -2\;\text{ns}$, the measured optimal phase $\phi_0(t_{\rm gap})$ slews linearly with gap duration, directly confirming the form of Eq. (10). Across all measured datasets where $A_1$ remains above $20\%$ of its plateau, the fitted slew rate $d\phi_0/dt_{\rm gap}$ tracks $70\text{–}83\%$ of the bare $2\pi\Delta$ prediction: $20.9\;\text{deg/ns}$ vs. $25.2\;\text{deg/ns}$ ($82.9\%$) at $\Delta = 70\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay_f1.h5`); $15.32 \pm 0.04\;\text{deg/ns}$ ($69.7\%$, rising to $17.56 \pm 0.19\;\text{deg/ns} = 79\%$ inside the collapse-free $[0, +3]\;\text{ns}$ window) at $\Delta = 61\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay.h5`, 60 phases); $20.70 \pm 0.07\;\text{deg/ns}$ ($77.7\%$) at $\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay2.h5`, 60 phases); and $74\text{–}79\%$ across low-$\Delta$ 16-phase sweeps (`delay_delta.h5`). The persistent $\approx 20\text{–}30\%$ effective detuning shortfall, $\delta_{\rm eff} \approx (0.21\text{–}0.30)\Delta$, is consistent across all runs and indicates nonlinear mode pulling or hybridization during free evolution. (An earlier apparent "zero-slew anomaly" at $\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$ in 16-phase data is retracted after 60-phase re-analysis showed it was an artifact of fitting into the noise-dominated collapsed tail.)
2. **Detuning-dependent memory decay $\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta)$:** In `delay_f1.h5` ($f_1 = 4066\text{–}4078\;\text{MHz}$, $\Delta = 64\text{–}76\;\text{MHz}$), $A_1(t_{\rm gap})$ remains on a $\sim 94\;\mu\text{W}$ plateau (band-mean readout) over $0\text{–}2\;\text{ns}$ before collapsing with a median exponential time constant $\tau_{\rm mem} = 2.4 \pm 0.7\;\text{ns}$ (independent DFT re-extraction: median $1.80\;\text{ns}$, IQR $1.28\text{–}2.09\;\text{ns}$ for $t_{\rm gap} \ge 1.5\;\text{ns}$). Extending the analysis across $\Delta = 50\text{–}88\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay_delta.h5`, $\Sigma = 4106\;\text{MHz}$) and high-resolution 60-phase sweeps (`delay.h5`, `delay2.h5`) reveals that **$\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta)$ depends strongly on detuning**:
   - **$\Delta = 50\text{–}58\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay_delta.h5`):** $32\text{–}36\%$ depth persists across the entire $7\;\text{ns}$ window with no measurable decay ($\tau_{\rm mem} > 7\;\text{ns}$ lower bound).
   - **$\Delta = 61\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay.h5`, 60 phases):** $A_1$ rises slightly from $46\;\mu\text{W}$ ($t_{\rm gap}<0$) to a $59\text{–}62\;\mu\text{W}$ plateau ($31\text{–}42\%$ depth) persisting undecayed to $\sim 6.5\;\text{ns}$ before decaying with late-time $\tau_{\rm mem} \approx 2.3\text{–}3.0\;\text{ns}$ and half-contrast time $t_{1/2} = 7.4\;\text{ns}$.
   - **$\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay2.h5`, 60 phases):** $A_1$ collapses rapidly from $111\;\mu\text{W}$ ($t_{\rm gap}=0$) with $\tau = 2.13 \pm 0.08\;\text{ns}$ ($t_{1/2} = 2.68\;\text{ns}$); the early drop is faster than exponential (Gaussian-onset $\sigma = 1.69\;\text{ns}$ yields lower $\chi^2_{\rm red}$), followed by a genuine $8\text{–}16\;\mu\text{W}$ ($6\text{–}12\sigma$) residual plateau over $4\text{–}9\;\text{ns}$.
   - **$\Delta \ge 80\;\text{MHz}$ (`delay_delta.h5`):** Initial depth drops to $4\text{–}16\%$ and vanishes within $<1\;\text{ns}$ ($\tau \approx 0.1\text{–}0.8\;\text{ns}$).

### E. Harmonic Structure and Mode-Pair Identity Switch (Fig. 5)

To identify why memory collapses near $\Delta \approx 74\;\text{MHz}$, Fig. 5 presents a model-free uniform 16-phase discrete Fourier transform (DFT) $c_n = (2/N)\sum_k I(\phi_k)e^{-in\phi_k}$ of `detune_fine.h5` ($20 \times 15$ grid in $(f_1, f_2)$, $t_{\rm gap}=0$ sequential pulses), cross-checked against `detune copy 2.h5` ($25$ phases).

Three findings emerge:
1. **Fundamental-harmonic dominance:** Across the strong-response ridge ($\Delta \approx 65\text{–}85\;\text{MHz}$, $\Sigma \approx 4106\text{–}4116\;\text{MHz}$; Figs. 5(a),(b),(f)), $c_1$ dominates with peak $A_1 = |c_1| = 117.2\;\mu\text{W}$ at $(4075, 4150)\;\text{MHz}$ (phase-mean $A_0 = 635\;\mu\text{W}$ at $(4065, 4154)\;\text{MHz}$), while $A_2/A_1 \le 0.30$ and $A_3/A_1 \le 0.25$ on the ridge (rising to $A_2/A_1 = 0.67$ only in the noise-limited high-$\Delta$ corner). Harmonics $n \ge 4$ are negligible (median $|c_{4..7}| = 6.4\%$ of median $A_1$; $\max A_4/A_1 = 0.20$ in the strong region), and a 3-harmonic truncation reconstructs $I(\phi)$ to $\le 3.8\%$ rms of $I_{\rm mean}$ (median $2.3\%$, Fig. 5(e)).
2. **Continuous $\arg c_1$:** Across the entire `detune_fine.h5` grid, $\arg c_1$ remains confined to $[-156^\circ, -148^\circ]$ ($\text{Re}\,c_1 < 0$ everywhere), showing no $\pi$ phase flip in the first harmonic.
3. **Second-harmonic $\pi$-jump boundary:** By contrast, $\arg c_2$ exhibits a sharp **$\sim\pi$ phase jump** (median step $132^\circ$ in `detune_fine.h5`, $143^\circ$ in `detune copy 2.h5`) along a straight line of constant detuning at **$\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$** (IQR $73.5\text{–}74.5\;\text{MHz}$, line rms $0.22\;\text{MHz}$ in `detune_fine.h5`; IQR $73.5\text{–}75.5\;\text{MHz}$, rms $0.72\;\text{MHz}$ in `detune copy 2.h5`; Fig. 5(d)), while $\arg c_1$ varies by $\le 1.4^\circ$ across the exact same pixels. In `delay2.h5` ($\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$), $A_2$ exhibits a sharp destructive dip in the co-drive window ($6.0\;\mu\text{W}$ at $[-1,0)\;\text{ns}$ vs. $19.7\;\mu\text{W}$ at $[-2,-1)\;\text{ns}$), confirming proximity to this boundary. Moreover, under a July operating point with tone 2 preceding tone 1 ($\Sigma \approx 4113\;\text{MHz}$, `delay_f1_JULY_varyf1a1.h5`), $\arg c_2$ flips at $\Delta \approx 76 \pm 2\;\text{MHz}$ and $\approx 103 \pm 2\;\text{MHz}$, demonstrating that the jump line tracks the interacting mode-pair resonance condition as $\Sigma$ shifts. We identify this $\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$ locus as a **mode-pair identity-switch boundary**, where the dominant participating pair $(k, m)$ switches across detuning.

### F. Turn-On Edge-Ramp Control and Synthesis Comparison (Fig. 6)

To test the assumption of an abrupt turn-on $t_{\rm on}$ in Eqs. (3) and (10), `meas5` varied the tone-2 Tukey edge width $t_{\rm edge}$ from $0.5$ to $10\;\text{ns}$ at the $\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$ operating point (`meas5_edges.h5`, Fig. 6(e)). The phase-averaged idler power $A_0$ remains nearly constant across the sweep, whereas the coherent modulation ratio $A_1/A_0$ drops monotonically from $0.38$ ($0.5\;\text{ns}$) and $0.35$ ($1\;\text{ns}$) to $0.19$ ($2\;\text{ns}$) and saturates at an $\approx 0.04\text{–}0.07$ floor for $t_{\rm edge} = 4\text{–}10\;\text{ns}$ (an end-of-run $1\;\text{ns}$ repeat reproduces $0.35$, excluding drift; $32\;\text{ns}$ edges in an earlier diagnostic run yielded $A_1/A_0 \le 0.005$ at $A_0 = 183\;\mu\text{W}$). Fitting $A_1(t_{\rm edge}) = A_{1,0}e^{-t_{\rm edge}/\tau_s} + c$ directly to the `meas5_edges.h5` traces gives $\tau_s = 1.87\;\text{ns}$ for $A_1$ ($\tau_s = 1.72\;\text{ns}$ for $A_1/A_0$, floor $\sim 8\;\mu\text{W}$ / $0.037$)—quantitatively matching both the $\Delta = 74\;\text{MHz}$ memory constant ($\tau_{\rm mem} = 2.13 \pm 0.08\;\text{ns}$) and the single-tone build-up time ($\tau_b = 2.15 \pm 0.08\;\text{ns}$). Whenever the turn-on ramp exceeds $\sim 2\;\text{ns}$, phase evolution during the ramp smears $\Psi$, erasing phase gating without suppressing total parametric power.

## V. Discussion and Outlook

### A. Resolving the Linewidth–Memory Tension and Far-Detuned Coherent Gating

Two quantitative tensions emerge from comparing our closed-form NMM predictions against experiment, both pointing to physics beyond small-signal linear steady states:

First, regarding relaxation timescales, once the $2\pi$ factor between Lorentzian HWHM ($\Gamma_{\rm Hz}$) and exponential amplitude rate ($\lambda = 2\pi\Gamma_{\rm Hz}$) is enforced consistently, the measured sub-threshold linewidths $\Gamma_{k,\rm Hz} = 1.50\text{–}1.61\;\text{MHz}$ agree closely with the NMM solver's intrinsic damping scale at $\alpha = 0.0005$ ($\Gamma_{\rm Hz} = 1.04\text{–}1.55\;\text{MHz}$; note that at $\alpha = 0.0020$ the solver ratio $\Gamma_{\rm Hz}/\alpha = (0.42\text{–}0.63)f_M$ gives $4.17\text{–}6.19\;\text{MHz}$). Under Eq. (11), the measured HWHM predicts a linear low-$\Delta$ memory time $\tau_{\rm mem}^{\rm lin} = (4\pi\Gamma_{k,\rm Hz})^{-1} = 49.4\text{–}53.1\;\text{ns}$ ($51.4\text{–}76.4\;\text{ns}$ at the solver's $\alpha=0.0005$ scale; $12.9\text{–}19.1\;\text{ns}$ at $\alpha=0.0020$). While the low-$\Delta$ lower bound ($\tau_{\rm mem} > 7\;\text{ns}$ at $\Delta \le 58\;\text{MHz}$; $t_{1/2} = 7.4\;\text{ns}$ at $\Delta = 61\;\text{MHz}$) remains compatible with $\tau_{\rm mem}^{\rm lin}$—with the decisive test `[PENDING meas1]` out to $t_{\rm gap} = 200\;\text{ns}$—the sharp collapse to $1.1\text{–}2.5\;\text{ns}$ at $\Delta = 74\text{–}78\;\text{MHz}$ and $<1\;\text{ns}$ above $80\;\text{MHz}$ is $20\text{–}30\times$ faster than linear damping and cannot be captured by static NMM branch-selection rules. Rather, the coincidence of $\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta = 74) \approx 2.13\;\text{ns}$, the edge-smearing scale $\tau_s \approx 1.8\;\text{ns}$, and the single-tone build-up time $\tau_b = 2.15\;\text{ns}$ ($\Gamma_{\rm dyn}^{\rm [Hz]} \approx 37\;\text{MHz}$) indicates that near the $\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$ identity-switch line, memory decay is governed by **nonlinear seed ring-down and multi-mode dephasing** of the driven seed state rather than small-signal relaxation.

Second, in CW steady-state theory [Eq. (3)], a cross-pump detuned by $\Delta = 50\text{–}80\;\text{MHz} \gg \Gamma_m$ averages out within tens of nanoseconds. Yet strong, clean rank-1 $\cos\phi$ gating is observed even when the $300\;\text{ns}$ and $400\;\text{ns}$ pulses overlap (Fig. 3, $\Delta \approx 58\text{–}64\;\text{MHz}$; Fig. 4(d) $t_{\rm gap} < 0$). We propose a **burst-wise attractor re-selection** mechanism: in every $1\text{–}2\;\mu\text{s}$ AWG loop, the spin-wave ensemble starts near thermal equilibrium (or the preceding seed state) and re-selects its parametric phase/amplitude basin [14] during the first few nanoseconds of tone-2 turn-on. Because the SA integrates emitted power over thousands of burst repetitions, an instantaneous turn-on bias $W\cos\Psi(t_{\rm on})$ tilts the basin-selection probability per burst even at large $\Delta$, restoring phase sensitivity far beyond the CW coherence limit $|\Delta| \lesssim \Gamma_m$. Direct pulse-width tomography (`[PENDING meas4]`) will discriminate this burst-wise mechanism from multi-pulse frequency-comb accumulation [15].

### B. Differentiation from Companion Two-Tone Steady-State Studies

Both the present study and the recent companion experiment by Soares *et al.* [11] investigate two-tone parallel pumping of a confined magnetic microdisk and test the coupled NMM framework of d'Aquino *et al.* [10]. However, the two works probe orthogonal physical axes:
1. **Discrete steady-state sequence vs. continuous phase-continuum memory:** Soares *et al.* resolve which discrete steady-state attractor ($z$, $u_h$, $u_n$, $c_{\pm\pm}$) the coupled mode pair settles into as a function of the *temporal ordering* (sequence) of two overlapping tones. Here, we resolve continuous memory of the **relative carrier phase $\phi$**—demonstrating $2\pi$-periodic gating ($\ge 50\%$ depth), continuous threshold modulation $h_{2,\rm th}(\Psi)$, and direct extraction of the internal cross-pump field $h_W = W/V_m$.
2. **Non-overlapping dark gap:** Whereas Ref. [11] operates in overlapping steady state, we introduce a programmable non-overlapping dark interval $t_{\rm gap}$ with $15.6\;\text{ps}$ resolution, proving that phase information propagates across the dark gap via $\phi_{\rm eff} = \phi + 2\pi\Delta\,t_{\rm gap}$ and decays with a detuning-dependent lifetime $\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta)$ bounded by a cross-dataset $c_2$ $\pi$-jump identity switch at $\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$.
3. **Material and readout regime:** Operating on an ensemble of BiYIG microdisks (the high-power instability family of Yemeli *et al.* [12]) with pulsed subharmonic $f/2$ readout naturally places the multi-attractor hopping of CW theory into the burst-wise probability-weighted regime of Sec. V.A, establishing phase-gated parametric memory as an isolation-free microwave control knob and a physical gating primitive for coherent spin-wave signal processing [16].

### C. Honest Limitations and Pending Campaign Tests

Three open caveats remain explicitly flagged: (i) the $20\text{–}30\%$ shortfall in $d\phi_0/dt_{\rm gap}$ relative to $2\pi\Delta$ ($\delta_{\rm eff} \approx (0.21\text{–}0.30)\Delta$) requires microscopic modeling of nonlinear frequency pulling [8] during seed free decay; (ii) absolute microscopic mode indices $k, m$ and panel Fig. 1(i) await the static-field FMR calibration and micromagnetic eigenmode fit (`[PENDING]`); and (iii) the overnight acquisition campaign on the setup PC will provide the definitive $200\;\text{ns}$ low-$\Delta$ memory curve (`[PENDING meas1]`, confirming `300/400 ns` pulse duration rather than legacy `4.69/6.25 ns` test scripts), the direct phase-dependent threshold curve $h_{2,\rm th}(\Psi)$ (`[PENDING meas2]`), and the 60-phase repeat-averaged gating depth at fixed operating point (`[PENDING meas3]`).

## VI. Conclusions

We have demonstrated phase-gated parametric amplification, cross-pump threshold control, and nanosecond phase memory between two detuned parallel-pumping tones in a BiYIG microdisk ensemble. By combining sub-threshold three-wave scattering ($P_{\rm id} \propto a_1^2 P_{f_2/2}$, self-consistently matching the $10\text{–}15\%$ $K_{2B}$ threshold drop) with a coherent four-magnon cross-pump ($W\cos\Psi$), the relative pump phase modulates the $f_2/2$ idler response with period $2\pi$ and $49\text{–}60\%$ depth. Across a dark propagation gap $t_{\rm gap}$, delay and phase act as a single physical control knob ($\phi_{\rm eff} = \phi + 2\pi\Delta\,t_{\rm gap}$), while the memory time $\tau_{\rm mem}(\Delta)$ transitions from $>7\;\text{ns}$ at low detuning ($\Delta \le 61\;\text{MHz}$) to $2.13\;\text{ns}$ at a cross-dataset second-harmonic $\pi$-jump boundary ($\Delta = 74.5\;\text{MHz}$), matching both the dynamic build-up time $\tau_b = 2.15\;\text{ns}$ and the turn-on edge-smearing scale $\tau_s \approx 1.8\;\text{ns}$. Together with our closed-form NMM threshold and gain relations, these results establish time-of-flight phase gating as a quantitative probe of nonlinear magnon dynamics in confined geometries.

### SM-S1. Sub-Threshold Self-Energy, Mirror Sidebands, and Coupling Constants

Writing the coupled rotating-frame equations for the seed-driven mode $k$ (pump $h_1$ at $f_1$) and an idler mode at $f_{\rm id,-} = f_1 - f_2/2$ driven by three-wave scattering $\kappa_{km} h_1$ (with target mode $m$ excited at $f_2/2$), adiabatic elimination of the idler amplitude when $|\kappa h_1| \ll \sqrt{\Gamma_{\rm id}^2 + \delta_{\rm id}^2}$ yields the complex self-energy $\Sigma_k = \kappa_{km}\kappa_{mk}^* h_1^2 / (\Gamma_{\rm id} + i\delta_{\rm id})$. Because $\phi_1$ cancels in $\kappa_{km}\kappa_{mk}^*$, $\Sigma_k$ shifts the effective damping $\Gamma_{\rm eff} = \Gamma_k - \text{Re}\,\Sigma_k$ and detuning $\delta' = \delta - \text{Im}\,\Sigma_k$ without phase sensitivity, giving Eq. (8). In the aligned symmetric limit ($\kappa_{km} = \kappa_{mk}$, $\beta_{mk}=1$, $\Gamma_k = \Gamma_{\rm id}$), symbolic algebra verifies $C_m \equiv K_{2B}$ identically (more generally $K_{2B}/C_m = \beta_{mk}^{-2}$). Under index exchange $1 \leftrightarrow 2$, $k \leftrightarrow m$, the upper sideband at $f_{\rm id,+} = f_2 - f_1/2$ obeys the exact mirror law $P_p \propto a_2^2 P_{f_1/2}$.

### SM-S2. Numerical Self-Tests and NMM Solver Verification

All closed-form equations—Eqs. (2), (4), (5), and (6)/(7)—pass exact eigenvalue bisection, algebraic steady-state, and unbiased fourth-order Runge–Kutta asymptotic growth-slope self-tests (`tmp/test_gamma_eqs.py`, relative error $< 10^{-6}$), as well as symbolic verification (`tmp/check_depth_criterion.py`, `tmp/check_2pi_convention.py`, `tmp/check_idler3.py`). Cross-validation against the full 29-mode NMM solver (`index.html`, `tmp/nmm_check_out.md`) confirms exact agreement for single-mode Arnold thresholds ($<3.5\times 10^{-14}$ relative deviation across 84 frequency/detuning grid points) and 16-phase thresholds ($2.1\times 10^{-11}\;\text{mT}$ max deviation once the mutual 4A shift $\Delta^{(4A)}_2 = n_{21}|a_1|^2$ is included).

### SM-S3. Methodological Note: Turn-On Edge Smearing and Data-Hygiene Traps

Phase-gating experiments require sharp pulse turn-on edges ($t_{\rm edge} \lesssim 1\;\text{ns}$) relative to the seed ring-up/memory scale ($\sim 2\;\text{ns}$). As shown in `meas5_edges.h5`, increasing $t_{\rm edge}$ to $4\text{–}32\;\text{ns}$ destroys $A_1/A_0$ while preserving $A_0$ (and thus can falsely mimic zero phase gating if pulse tapers are over-smoothed). Similarly, legacy delay acquisitions store both sample-path (`ro = 1`) and monitor (`ro = 1EX`, $60\;\text{MHz}$ span) keys and exhibit $\sim 3\text{–}8\%$ baseline-recalibration steps within shuffled phase blocks; all results reported here isolate `ro = 1` and apply automated change-point/outlier rejection.
